Galton Board_

An illustrated look at random paths and the distribution they create.

~/galton-board

[ diagram ]

~/how-it-works

[ read ]

A Galton board sends balls through rows of pins. Each collision can move a ball left or right; its final bin reflects the combined path.

Why the center fills up

Many different paths contain roughly equal numbers of left and right turns. Only one path goes left every time, and one goes right every time. Central outcomes therefore have more possible paths.

A simple mathematical model

If every turn is independent and equally likely, the number of right turns follows a binomial distribution. With eight turns, there are 256 equally likely left/right sequences.

P(k right turns) = C(8, k) / 2⁸

The physical simulation can differ from this ideal model because of collision geometry, velocities, and removed pins.

About the original experiment

The original version explored the number of pin rows, ball radius, and ball count. This JavaScript-free page keeps an illustration of the idea; it does not run the simulation.
